# EpiTrace: Title Candidates & Selection

## Five candidates

| # | Title | Semantic space |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **EpiTrace: From Scientific Claims to Epistemic Execution Constraints** | mechanism-oriented; names the input→output pipeline |
| 2 | EpiTrace: Verifying the Scientific Validity of Machine-Learning Executions | outcome-oriented; "scientific validity" is broad |
| 3 | Beyond Reproducibility: Verifying the Evidential Validity of ML Experiments | framing-oriented; positions vs. reproducibility |
| 4 | When Correct Numbers Are Not Valid Evidence | claim-oriented; the conceptual thesis |
| 5 | Scientific Execution Conformance for Machine Learning | field-oriented; names the problem class |

## Selection

**Chosen: Candidate 1 — "EpiTrace: From Scientific Claims to Epistemic
Execution Constraints."**

Rationale (novelty defensibility + clarity, not drama):
- Candidate 1 names the *mechanism* (claims → epistemic constraints), which
  is exactly the defensible novel axis and immediately distinguishes EpiTrace
  from provenance/workflow systems in Candidate 2/5's generic space.
- Candidate 2 ("scientific validity") is too broad and invites the
  "you're not proving the science" objection (our §9 limitation 6);
  Candidate 1's "epistemic execution constraints" is precise and bounded.
- Candidate 3 ("Beyond Reproducibility") is a strong *framing* but the word
  "beyond" is a marketing-adjacent claim; keep it as a Related-Work frame,
  not the title.
- Candidate 4 is the best *conceptual* statement but too abstract to carry
  the mechanism; use it as the abstract's thesis sentence.
- Candidate 5 is the cleanest *problem-class* name but undersells the
  paper→constraint induction contribution.

Candidate 1 balances defensibility (bounded claim) with clarity (names the
pipeline). "Epistemic execution constraints" is a coined term the paper
defines in §2, giving the title a stable referent.
